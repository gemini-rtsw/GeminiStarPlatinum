What this is

A telescope you can feel the physics of

Every movement in the simulation is the result of physics constraints — drive springs, velocity drives, and locked anchor bodies — rather than baked animation. The longer-term intent is to feed it real data from a TCS (Telescope Control System) EPICS interface so the virtual observatory tracks the real one.

🛰️

Constraint-driven motion

Azimuth, elevation, and Cassegrain rotation are produced by UPhysicsConstraintComponent drives chasing target angles each tick.

🌌

The observatory dome

A second physics actor twists the dome, opens its shutter, and works its vents — sharing the same velocity-then-snap drive pattern as the mount.

📡

Live-feed ready

A coordinator and data feed are scaffolded to switch between Manual and Live control and broker a real EPICS telescope feed.

Deliberate & fragile

Physics conventions worth respecting

⚡

The FluxCapacitor

A zero-mass helper body spun forever by a velocity drive — its only job is to stop UE's sleep system from halting slow, precise rotations. Do not remove it.

🎯

Velocity-then-snap

Drives push with a velocity drive past a threshold, then snap to an orientation spring within it. Angles are unwound to [-180°, 180°] to mimic real mount limits.

⚖️

Tuned masses

Anchors weigh ~1e29 to act as immovable references; center-of-mass offsets come straight from SolidWorks mass-property data, mm converted to cm.

⚠ Known bug: setting elevation mass too high fails to stabilize. Masses are load-bearing tuned values — treat them with care.
